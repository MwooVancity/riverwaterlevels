# River Water Levels (riverwaterlevels.com)

Lakebrief-style, ad-supported US river gauge site. One page per USGS gauge (conditions, forecast where NWS issues one, history and flood record, jump links), river hubs, state pages, trust pages. Government data only. Domain bought 2026-10-16, served by a Cloudflare Worker with static assets. Pre-launch noindex hold: lifted ONLY when the owner says so.
This folder is its own project: the car-repair site (calcscrape) rules do not apply here. Inherited on purpose from the owner's global rules: gates are code, not notes; "verified" means the artifact was read in the same turn; a fix that needs a third iteration has the wrong target, so redefine what the rule matches.

## Line 0: the reader first (HARD, owner 2026-10-18: "make that line 0")
Every decision, page, block, widget, rule and release is judged by one question: **does a person standing at the river, on a phone, understand every condition of this river in seconds and trust it?** Correct is necessary, never enough.
- The first screen answers without scrolling: how much water, how it compares with normal, which way it is moving, what is coming (forecast, alerts, rain, thunderstorms), how old the data is.
- Study the best competing pages as screenshots, then design; look at every change at 390 px and 1280 px, light and dark, before it ships.
- Widgets over tables where a picture is faster; tables keep the exact numbers and sources. Plain words, units everywhere, abbreviations explained, a safety line next to the numbers.
- Every feature is tested against a person: a paddler, an angler, a flood watcher, a first-time visitor. If a page serves the rules but not the person, the rules change.
- Users are water-sports people (paddling, fishing, rafting, tubing, swimming, boating). The release order, the blocks and the missing-data lines are judged by what they need to decide.

## Gates that stop a deploy (HARD; code, not notes)
`refresh.py` runs: collect, build, `check.py`, `tools/template_hash.py --check`, `ui_gates.mjs`, `publish.py`, deploy, `livecheck.py`, then `tools/truth_live.py` (logged). Any failing step before the deploy leaves the live site on its last good release.
- `check.py` (16 broken fixtures in `--selftest` must all fail): source line per block, units and gauge-local time, first-screen tiles, no jargon in headings, no empty widget, images sized with alt, heading order, no repeated sentence, secrets scan, structured data valid, meta and Open Graph, sentinel values banned, page weight (gauge page over 90 KB compressed fails), sitemap equals the indexable set, indexing config and `_headers` agree.
- `tests.py`: determinism, stale source, rollback, unit logic, hidden blocks, review gate, signed verdicts, kill switch, related-gauge closure, truth tests. `truth_tests.py`: trend direction, comparison words, silence, stage-marker time, plain words, storm line, forecast units, datum note, symmetric related gauges, plausible numbers (contract 19: no negative non-tidal flow, impossible temperature). `truth_types.py` (blocks a deploy): nine gauge-type variants of a real cache (no statistics, no stage, no optional source, no alert data, tidal swing, flat sensor, zero flow, three readings, gap) must render without None/NaN/undefined and a 40-day-old sensor must be refused. `tools/truth_live.py --dir dist_next` writes `_truth.json` into the build (status ok/skipped/mismatch, last_ok_at); an NWS outage no longer blocks a deploy, it is recorded as skipped; `tools/watchdog.py` fails when the numbers were not verified against USGS and NWS for 6 h or a mismatch was found.
- `ui_gates.mjs` (390 px first screen, tap targets, contrast, text size, no sideways scroll, accessibility, keyboard focus, layout shift, 400 kbit/s load); CI installs Playwright for it. `render_test.mjs` and `js_test.mjs` test the published `dist/`, run them after a publish. Hourly `watchdog.yml` on the live site: stale build, stale readings or too few pages fails and emails the owner.
- **Review gate (HARD, owner 2026-10-19: "look at every page and rip it apart ... user first, not Google ... before it deploys"):** a NEW gauge page does not go live, and a template change does not deploy, without a SHIP verdict from a fresh-brain reader review (reviewers get no hints). `tools/record_review.py` writes the verdict WITH a signature tied to the report file; a hand-edited verdict, an edited report or a verdict for another URL does not count (`lib/reviewgate.py`). Unreviewed new pages are held out of the build while the rest still ships; `build.py --no-hold` includes them for review builds. GRANDFATHERED (the 25 pilot pages live before the gate) never unlocks indexing. Every mechanical defect a review finds becomes an automated gate the same day.
- **Indexing is a kill switch, not a header (HARD):** `data/index-allow.json` ({"open", "site_pages", "allow"}, read by `lib/indexing.py`). Hold (`open` false): every production host sends `X-Robots-Tag: noindex` and every page carries a robots noindex tag. Open: the production header rule is dropped, only allowed gauge pages are indexable, `site_pages` controls the non-gauge pages, and `sitemap.xml` lists only indexable pages. `check.py` refuses a build when the config and `_headers` disagree, when an allowed page lacks a signed SHIP, or when the sitemap differs. `python tools/open_wave.py N [--apply]` picks the next N live, signed-SHIP pages by `data/waves.csv` that passed the queue gate, render at least 15 data blocks and have 10 years of record; `--pull URL` takes pages back. Releasing is slow to undo (search engines act only when they recrawl): small batches, measure each, never deploy pages that will stay noindex for weeks.

## Hard rules
- **No composed prose or verdicts on data pages.** Visible text is a source value with unit and time, a label from `lib/render.py`, or a verbatim source string (escaped). The thunderstorm line states only what the NWS forecast text says. A runnable or fishable range may be shown only as a verbatim, attributed, dated source value.
- **Every block has a `Source:` line** (what, who, when). A block with no data returns `None` (no heading, no source line); the gaps line says what is missing for the gauge so silence never reads as "fine".
- **Terms gating:** a block renders only if its source in `data/sources.json` is `quoted` or `owner-permission`; `off` sources are built but never shown. New source: search for its terms (WebSearch, not WebFetch), quote them in `sources.json`, then build. Public-domain federal status is recorded, not assumed.
- **Freshness (the code is the arbiter, `lib/render.py`):** flow older than 24 h shows "Gauge not reporting"; older than 30 days that gauge is not rebuilt (its last page stays live, the build continues); river forecast amber at 24 h and hidden at 72 h; weather obs 3 h / 12 h; hourly forecast amber at 12 h, hidden at 72 h; the Methodology page states these limits and must be edited with them; rain 6 h / 24 h; alerts amber at 3 h; SNOTEL 5 days; CPC 3 days. The NWS forecast is read by units, never by column position; when NWS and USGS read a gauge on different zero points (differ by more than 0.3 ft) the page says so and measures flood stages against the NWS reading.
- **`collect.py` is the only code that touches the network.** `build.py` reads `cache/` only. A failed source keeps its previous cache value. Gate files live in `data/`, never `state/` (the CI cache restores `state/`).
- **Percentile** = interpolation of USGS daily percentiles for the date, shown as "estimated"; never presented as a USGS value. Time zones: gauge-local with abbreviation. Changes (1 h, 6 h, 24 h) use means over short windows so one noisy reading cannot set a headline.
- Upstream / downstream pairs are listed on both pages (`lib/related.py`). A live gauge's address never changes (name fixes skip live gauges).
- No API keys in pages, zips or docs. The key lives in the calcscrape `.env`, read only by `lib/common.py`.
- Owner is a non-coder: do the work, plain short replies, never ask them to run commands. Verify live pages before saying "live". One key-scanned zip for ChatGPT, built only when everything is done. The user's global rule to end a substantive turn with "Next: ..." applies here (an old note in the plan said not to; the global instruction is newer and stronger).

## Pipeline
`python -I collect.py [site..]` (default the 25 pilot gauges) -> `python -I build.py` (to `dist_next/`; `--preview` shows uncleared blocks, `--no-hold` includes unreviewed pages) -> `python -I check.py` -> `python -I publish.py` (atomic swap to `dist/`, previous in `dist_prev/`, `--rollback`) -> `npx wrangler deploy` -> `python -I livecheck.py`. `python -I refresh.py [--fast]` runs all of it with a lock (`runs/refresh.lock`) and log (`runs/refresh.log`); for manual deploys always use it so nothing collides with the scheduler. Collector modes: `--fast` (bulk USGS latest incl. related gauges, NWS, NWPS, rain: every 30 min), `--nws-only`, `--extras-only [--keys=a,b]`; a full collect only every 6 h. USGS rate-limits bursts (429).
Deployer: GitHub Actions `refresh.yml` (30 min fast, 6 h full, installs Playwright) and `watchdog.yml` (hourly). Two Windows tasks ("RiverWaterLevels Fast Refresh", "RiverWaterLevels Refresh") still deploy from the owner's PC and will be disabled once the watchdog has run clean for a day (two deployers can race; `tests.py` step 4 can flake).
Planning tools (not site build): `tools/fetch_geo.py` (coordinates, Wild and Scenic and Nationwide Rivers lines, Census places into `data/geo/`), `tools/clean_names.py` (name cleaner v2 into `data/name-fixes.csv`), `tools/demand.py` (Wikipedia views, a tiebreak only), `tools/rescore.py` (release order into `data/waves.csv` and `data/waves-report.txt`, back-test `data/backtest.txt`), `tools/open_wave.py`, `tools/record_review.py`, `tools/template_hash.py`, `tools/truth_live.py`, `tools/watchdog.py`, `tools/serve.mjs` (serves a build for review). Review build: `build.py --no-hold`, copy `dist_next` to `dist_review`, `node tools/serve.mjs dist_review 8123`, stop the server afterwards.

## Page template (frozen "final form")
Gauge page, top to bottom: alert banner when NWS has an active alert; stat tiles (flow and gage height with 24 h sparklines; comparison with past years in plain words with a dial; trend from the last 6 h; alerts only when there are none; forecast high; water and air temperature; upstream tile; rain; sunset; "Your range" last, `MYLV_JS`: the reader's own low and high flow saved in the browser only); a gaps line (what is not available for this gauge, dams upstream); jump links; what is happening now and next (alerts, 7 days, gage height with flood-stage ladder, flood impacts, rate of rise); when NWS issues a river forecast, a **Forecast** section (river forecast table, National Water Model guidance, rain totals, outlooks, hourly forecast with the thunderstorm line, weather, radar within reach, air observation, sun), otherwise hourly forecast, weather and radar; water quality, water temperature by month, SNOTEL; heading "How today compares with past years" (30 days, 12 months, same 50 days in earlier years, comparison with the record); three CLOSED groups (`<details class="fold">`): Flood record and history, Daily values, measurements and rating, Watershed, water quality and land (a jump link, permalink or #anchor opens the group holding it; printing opens all). Right column (below the content on a phone): upstream and downstream strip, other gauges nearby (up to five within 60 miles), NWS forecast point, map, official pages, about the gauge. Tools row: Save gauge, CSV download. Phone: one column, tables with four or more visible columns become stacked rows, jump links scroll sideways. A failed live update says "live update unavailable"; the live script retires the static "already above this forecast" note when a newer stage arrives. Old `/water/` and `/forecast/` addresses redirect to the gauge page. Not built: unit toggle.
