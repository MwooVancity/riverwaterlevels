## What this is
riverwaterlevels.com: an ad-supported US river gauge site. **One page per USGS gauge** (conditions, forecast where NWS issues one, history and flood record on one page with jump links), river hubs, state pages, trust pages. Government data only, a Source line on every block, a block shows only when its source terms are cleared. Domain registered 2026-10-16, served by a Cloudflare Worker with static assets. noindex hold stays until the owner says so (kill switch: `data/index-allow.json`).

## Numbers (checked 2026-10-18)
- Inventory 9,099 gauges. Gate (`tools/rescore.py`) lets 7,832 through (7,549 queued + 283 held for names) and excludes 1,242: 546 tiny basins (under 5 sq mi), 490 with under 3 years of record (392 of them have no statistics at all), 206 ditch / canal / drain / floodway names. 25 are live.
- Pages at full size: about 7,860 gauge pages + 1,545 river hubs + 51 states + about 12 trust / status pages = about 9,500 files, well under the 20,000 limit. **"Tens of thousands of pages" cannot come from gauges alone.** More pages need another page type with its own reader question: lakes and reservoirs (decided in), and possibly NWS flood forecast points that have no USGS gauge. Pay for Workers ($5 a month, 100,000 files) only if a page type is proven first.
- Pilot pages weigh 28 to 55 KB compressed (140 to 180 KB raw HTML); `check.py` fails a gauge page above 90 KB compressed. The old "150 KB" bar was for raw HTML and is withdrawn.
- Deploy cost: about 11 to 12 files a second were uploaded in the pilot. At 9,500 files a full deploy is roughly 15 minutes: tail gauges must refresh daily, not every 30 minutes (design below, not built).

## Release queue (how the order is decided)
`python -I tools/fetch_geo.py` (once) then `python -I tools/rescore.py` writes `data/waves.csv` and `data/waves-report.txt`.
Score = gate x (0.45 Activity + 0.15 Reach + 0.25 Decision + 0.15 Quality) minus a duplicate penalty; weights were set after a back-test (the reviewer proposed 0.35 / 0.25 / 0.25 / 0.15; reach by population under-ranked rural recreation towns such as Klamath, Copperhill and West Yellowstone).
Activity: Wild and Scenic segment within 3 km (recreation value 100, fish value 90, any 90), Nationwide Rivers Inventory segment 60 (description mentions paddling 85, fishing 75), hand-list river in a named state 70 or 100, tailwater below a dam 65, size band 30. The hand list `data/recreation-rivers.txt` is state-qualified for generic names. Reach: Census places (population, distance) and a validated Wikipedia river article. Decision: NWS forecast point, tailwater, flashy size band. Quality: record years and name confidence. Duplicate: second gauge of a river loses 10 unless its drainage is 2x a better gauge, further gauges lose 10 more; at most one gauge of a river per release day.
Back-test (`data/backtest.txt`, 25 well-known gauges chosen by the planner, must be live or in the first 1,500 queue positions = 60 days): 19 of 25. **This is not a valid out-of-sample test**: the weights were adjusted after seeing which of those 25 missed (the fame list was also state-qualified and the weights moved to favour activity), so the 19 is a tuned result. A valid test needs a gauge list fixed before tuning, from a source other than the planner (outfitter and club pages, state fishing sites, American Whitewater river lists); the audit prompt asks for exactly that list. Known misses and why: Snoqualmie, Puyallup, Buffalo River at Ponca, Bighorn at Kane, South Platte at Cheesman (no Wild and Scenic / Inventory line near the gauge, no boat-ramp or public-land signal yet); the Blue River backtest entry has no gauge in the inventory.
Not yet in the score because no free bulk source has been read: boat ramps and put-ins (Recreation.gov RIDB needs a free key), PAD-US public land, state trout lists, flow variability per gauge (needs the full history, collected at stage S4). After the first cohort is indexed, replace the weights with Search Console impressions per gauge.
Names: `tools/clean_names.py` (name cleaner v2) fixed 1,383 of the 1,801 low-confidence names (`data/name-fixes.csv`); 283 remain held and are reviewed by hand in score order. Live gauges are never renamed (their addresses must not change).

## Release stages
Stages S0 to S6 and their gates are in `docs/RISKS-AND-STAGES.md` with corrected page counts. A page is released only if it is live, has a signed SHIP review, passed the queue gate, renders at least 15 data blocks and has 10 or more years of record (`tools/open_wave.py`, thresholds re-set after a sample of tail gauges has been built).

## Gates that block a deploy (all in code)
`check.py` (first screen, units, sources, structure, secrets, page weight, indexing config), `tests.py` (determinism, stale source, rollback, unit logic, review gate, signatures, kill switch, related-gauge closure), `truth_tests.py` (trend direction, comparison words, silence, stage marker time, plain words, storm line, forecast units, datum note, symmetric related gauges), `tools/template_hash.py --check` (the current template needs a signed SHIP review), `ui_gates.mjs` (390 px first screen, tap targets, contrast, accessibility, layout shift, slow connection; now blocking and run in CI). After the deploy, not blocking: `tools/truth_live.py` (live pages against USGS and NWS) and the hourly `watchdog.yml`.
A review verdict counts only when `tools/record_review.py` signed it against the report file (`lib/reviewgate.py`); a hand-edited verdict, an edited report or a wrong URL is refused.

## Water-sports content
Built: flow, stage with NWS flood stages, comparison with past years in plain words, trend, forecast high, alerts, thunderstorm line (hours the NWS hourly forecast calls thunderstorms, or an explicit "none"), rain, air and water temperature where a sensor exists, upstream / downstream strip with measured lag, "Your range" tile (reader's own band).
Researched 2026-10-18 with WebSearch, not built: Reclamation RISE (Glen Canyon and other release series; catalog marks them public, no licence text found), USACE CWMS Data API (API docs public domain, data terms not stated), National Inventory of Dams (metadata states no special restrictions, download rate limits), American Whitewater flow ranges (no terms of use found: do not copy; link only). Federal works are generally public domain, but the register in `data/sources.json` keeps these hidden until an owner-sent email or a quoted terms page is on file. Spec for the release block: dam-to-gauge mapping from NLDI plus gauge name, RISE or CWMS series for scheduled and actual release, shown only where a series exists, with the series time and a "scheduled, not guaranteed" label.
Not built, needs a source: runnable / fishable / float ranges (only as verbatim, attributed, dated source values), bacteria and algae advisories (state / EPA), low-head dam statement near known structures, boat ramps and put-ins (RIDB), cold-water warning thresholds (only if a named agency publishes one).

## Review rounds on the merged template (2026-10-18/19)
Thirteen fresh-brain reviews (`docs/research/FRESH-REVIEW-10-A` to `-M`, reader-first, each with cross-checks against live USGS and NWS) plus the plan review. Values matched the agencies on every gauge checked in rounds D to M; what they found was wording, silence, contradictions between two statements of the same thing, claims on trust pages and, twice, real data gaps (a forecast table that cut off its own peak; a thinned 15-minute series from the fast collect). Every class became a truth test (17 contracts across all 25 gauges: direction, bands, silence, stage marker time, plain words, storm line, forecast units, datum note, symmetric related gauges, noisy flow, forecast offset, one number one source for 6 h and 24 h changes, forecast high in the table, dams always stated, NWS impact quoted at the current stage, no advice wording). Round M found no P0 and its template was signed SHIP; two small follow-ups (N, O: phone tables) were reviewed and signed the same way, and GitHub Actions deployed the final template through every gate (live, verified 2026-10-19: 49 pages, redirects 301, noindex header on, `truth_live` 0 mismatches, `js_test` and `render_test` pass).
Open from the last rounds (P1, need a new template review to ship): the declarations table hides its Programs column on phones; impact statement is small text far down on a phone and a rule overlaps it; no plain "no NWS impact at this level" line; the site's own instruction-like sentences ("follow official warnings") versus the Methodology claim of no advice; only gauges named "below ... dam" get the below-a-dam wording; headline "Steady" beside large 12 h release swings; upstream tile can show a tributary; "forecast high" below the current reading; mobile has no menu; header search ranks "colorado" third.

## Gotchas
USGS rate-limits bursts (429): never several full collects an hour. `collect.py` is the only network code. Gate files live in `data/`, not `state/` (the CI cache restores `state/`). `build.py --no-hold` includes unreviewed new pages for review builds. A scheduled refresh can race manual tests (tests.py step 4 can flake). Never write backslash code through a shell heredoc; use Write / Edit. Read screenshots: browser tests do not catch every visual bug.
