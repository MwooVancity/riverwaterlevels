## Findings, ranked by ranking impact

### 1. No river hub for any of the 25 gauges; "<river> water level" has no landing page
Evidence: `lib/pages.py:30-36` gives every row a `hub_url` (`/river/<hub_slug>/`) and `build.py:99` emits `directory.hub_page` only when more than one gauge of that river is on the site. The sitemap (49 URLs, curled this session) contains zero `/river/<hub>/` URLs; every gauge row is `_single`, so `lib/page.py:293` drops the river from the breadcrumb. `data/pages-all.csv` shows the same rivers have 2 (New), 3 (Gauley), 12 (Missouri), 18 (Arkansas), 17 (Chattahoochee) gauges in the inventory. The reader typing "new river water level" means the river, not Thurmond; Google has nothing on this domain whose H1 is "New River". The home page H1 is "River levels and flows for US rivers and creeks" and state H1s are "Rivers in West Virginia: 3 rivers, 3 gauges".
Fix: ship the second gauge for each of the 25 rivers so `hub_page` exists, then make the hub a real page: the river's gauges top to bottom with flow, percentile, 24-h change, NWS forecast, and the hub's H1 "<River> water level and flow" with the headwater-to-mouth table in server HTML. The gauge page keeps "<River> at <Place>". Effort: medium (release.py already handles gauges; hub needs the headline tiles the gauge page already renders).

### 2. The title does not contain the query words and carries no number
Evidence: `lib/page.py:63` `"title": "%s, %s: river level and flow"` renders `<title>New River at Thurmond, WV: river level and flow</title>`. The reader's queries are "water level", "flow", "cfs", "level today". The body uses "water level" 5 times, all in boilerplate (og:image alt and the sources footer); "river level" 3 times. snoflo's title: `Gauley River Above Belva Flow: 829 cfs · West Virginia USGS 03192000`. Google's essentials: "Use words that people would use to look for your content" (developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials). H1 is `New River at Thurmond, WV` with no noun at all.
Fix: title `New River water level at Thurmond, WV: <flow> cfs, <stage> ft (USGS 03185400)`; H1 `New River at Thurmond, WV: water level and flow`; meta description leads with the reading and its time (the page already rebuilds every 30 min, so the number is honest). Putting the reading in the title is what snoflo does and it is the only templated site in these SERPs. One line in `page.py:63`, re-check `check.py:144-155` duplicate-title rule still passes. Effort: small.

### 3. No publisher, no contact, no person: the E-E-A-T gap the reviewer already flagged
Evidence: `data/publisher.json` `{"name": "River Water Levels", "email": "", "waived": "owner 2026-10-20 ... the reviewer rated this the top quality-rater risk (FRESH-REVIEW-10-V P1)"}`; `lib/directory.py:193-203` prints "Publisher contact: not yet listed; the owner has waived it for this first release." on the About page (read live this session). JSON-LD has WebPage + WebSite + BreadcrumbList and no Organization/Person, no `publisher`, no `sameAs`. Google's helpful-content self-assessment: "Is it self-evident to your visitors who authored your content?", "Is the use of automation, including AI-generation, self-evident to visitors through disclosures or in other ways?" (developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content). Competitors: USGS/NWS are .gov; snoflo names "Snoflo.org LLC"; AW is a nonprofit.
Fix: a real contact (an email on the domain), a named operator on /about/ with one sentence on who builds it and how (automated from USGS/NWS, reviewed by whom), `Organization` with `name`, `url`, `email`, `sameAs` in the WebSite JSON-LD and `publisher` on every WebPage. Effort: small; needs the owner's name/email only.

### 4. Internal links: one neighbour, no river, no cross-state neighbours
Evidence: `href="/river/..."` on the Thurmond page resolves to exactly one other gauge (Gauley above Belva) plus `/rivers/wv/`; the Gauley page links back to Thurmond only. "Nearby gauges" and "Upstream/Downstream forecast points" sections exist but list NWS points, not pages on this site (`lib/related.py` only closes pairs that are both on the site). snoflo links five nearby USGS gauges plus paddle runs and launches. Google: "Make your links crawlable" and crawl depth drives discovery for a new domain.
Fix: on every gauge page, a "Nearby gauges on this site" block with the 5 nearest by distance regardless of river/state, with their live flow (data is already in `gauges.json`, served to the search box); the state page card for each river; the hub. Each new release wave then links itself in without hand work. Effort: small (one block, haversine over gauges.json at build).

### 5. State pages are 1-3 cards and index pages, not answers
Evidence: `/rivers/wv/` (46.8 KB) H1 "Rivers in West Virginia: 3 rivers, 3 gauges", body = three cards + a search box (`lib/directory.py:139-154`). Twelve of sixteen state pages have 1 gauge. These are the pages most exposed to "thin" and "doorway" readings: "Doorway abuse is when sites or pages are created to rank for specific, similar search queries. They lead users to intermediate pages that aren't as useful as the final destination" (developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies).
Fix: until a state has 5+ gauges, keep it `noindex,follow` (one flag in `data/index-allow.json` logic: `site_pages` is site-wide today, `lib/indexing.py:22-27`; it needs a per-state threshold). When indexed, the state page should be a table with live flow, percentile, 24-h change, alert flag for every gauge, sortable, plus the state's NWS active river alerts count. Effort: small for the threshold, medium for the table.

### 6. 20,000 pages is only safe if each page's data is visibly original and the rollout follows demand
Evidence: Google spam policy: "Scaled content abuse is when many pages are generated for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings and not helping users ... typically focused on creating large amounts of unoriginal content that provides little to no value to users, no matter how it's created"; example: "Scraping feeds, search results, or other content to generate many pages ... where little value is provided to users". The helpful-content doc asks "Does the content provide substantial value when compared to other pages in search results?" and "Does the content provide original information, reporting, research, or analysis?" What separates Lakebrief/snoflo-style pages from a USGS mirror is exactly the analysis: percentile vs the gauge's own record for the date, change over 1-24 h, "last time this high", travel lag between gauges, alert status, all computed and shown with the source and the time (About page, read live). Our pages do that; the inventory in `data/inventory-ranked.csv` and `data/demand.csv` already rank by demand.
Risk signals on our side: boilerplate share is high (788 of ~1,200 unique text lines shared between Thurmond and Gauley, mostly CSS/JS/legend text but visible to a rater as identical paragraphs: "Government data, shown with the time it was observed or issued. It is not a flood warning..."), 12 one-gauge state pages, and 9,100 inventory rows that include names like "Sacramento River A Freeport" (`pages-all.csv` river column = place column, shown live in the sitemap slug `sacramento-river/freeport` is fine but the inventory row is not).
Fix: release in demand order with the hub-first rule (#1), cap the shared paragraphs to one short disclosure per page, never index a gauge whose USGS record lacks daily statistics (no percentile = no original analysis = a mirror). Effort: policy, no code beyond #1 and #5.

### 7. lastmod: every one of the 49 URLs says 2026-10-21, including /privacy/ and /disclaimer/
Evidence: sitemap curled this session; `build.py:109-118` writes `lastmod` from a content hash per build, so any build-wide change (nav, footer, gauge count) moves every page's date; gauge pages change every 30 min by design. Google: "The `<lastmod>` value should reflect the date and time of the last significant update to the page" and Google uses it only "if it's consistently and verifiably" accurate (developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/build-sitemap). A date that is always today is a date Google learns to ignore, which removes the one freshness signal a live-data site should own.
Fix: hash only `<main>` minus live spans (`data-live`, age chips, timestamps) for trust/state/home pages so their lastmod moves on real edits; for gauge pages use the observation time of the latest reading (true "significant update"), also emitted as `dateModified` (already `iso(G["lt"])`, `lib/page.py:299`) so sitemap and JSON-LD agree. Effort: small.

### 8. Schema: skip Dataset, add Place; keep BreadcrumbList
Evidence: Google's Dataset doc: "The purpose of this markup is to improve discovery of datasets" (Dataset Search), "Google does not guarantee that features that consume structured data will show up in search results", and no statement that it affects Search ranking (developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/dataset); secondary coverage says Dataset markup now serves Dataset Search only. snoflo carries Place + GeoCoordinates + PostalAddress; USGS carries GovernmentOrganization + GeoCoordinates. Our BreadcrumbList mirrors the visible crumb (`lib/page.py:231`), which is the only one with a documented Search feature.
Fix: add `Place` (`name`, `geo`, `containedInPlace` state) and `publisher` Organization to the WebPage; do not add Dataset (no Search benefit, extra bytes). Effort: small. Value: low-medium (entity clarity, not rank).

### 9. Activity thresholds are the missing "why" for obscure gauges
Evidence: on Kinzua and Penns the SERP is forums quoting thresholds ("above 3.1 feet for paddling", "400 cfs is a good wading level"). Our pages show the reading and percentile but no activity band for the gauge; the Line 0 rule in the repo CLAUDE.md ("every data block for every water activity") asks for this. American Whitewater publishes gauge-to-run ranges per river.
Fix: a "What this flow means" row from a public, citable table (AW gauge ranges where they exist; NWS flood categories already shown; NPS conversion tables where published) with the source link. Effort: medium (one fetch script per source).

### 10. Technical state is good; keep it
Evidence: 200, canonical correct, robots allow all, sitemap present, HSTS, CSP report-only, no external scripts/CSS, all readings present in the initial HTML (`data-live` spans are server-filled; JS only refreshes), 0.3-0.4 s TTFB-to-complete on 210 KB. Google: "If the content isn't visible in the rendered HTML, Google won't be able to index it"; ours is in the raw HTML. Two defects: `llms.txt` 404 (harmless for Google, cheap to add for AI crawlers), and inline CSS+JS repeated on every page (~40 KB each) which is bytes not rank.

## Indexing reality for 25 pages opened today
A new domain with no links is crawled from the sitemap at Google's pace: days to weeks for the first crawl, more for indexing, and impressions follow a further lag; third-party estimates range from days to months and none is authoritative. Three things that move it: (1) Search Console verification plus sitemap submission plus "Request indexing" on the 25 gauge URLs and the home page; (2) a handful of real links (the state's paddling club, a fishing forum profile, AW's gauge page comments, a county emergency-management resource page), because a domain with zero links is crawled last; (3) honest lastmod so Googlebot learns which pages change. IndexNow is Bing/Yandex/Seznam/Naver only; Google does not support it; use it anyway for Bing (one POST per build) but expect nothing from Google. `tools/index_live.py` exists; wire it to the Search Console API once verified.
